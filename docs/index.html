Why the red frame went: red is the colour we most want to measure in plants (anthocyanin). A large saturated red area next to the subject competes with it in segmentation and pulls the camera's automatic white balance.

Why the v1.5 colours were replaced: pure screen blue, red and cyan are outside what a sticker press can print, so the printed patch would not match its file value. They also don't correspond to anything a plant or soil scientist records.

How the colours were chosen

Every chip is a Munsell notation (hue value/chroma, e.g. 7.5YR 5/6), the colour language soil scientists already use in the field and the basis of the Munsell Plant Tissue charts. The notation is the design; the printed colour is computed from it, then measured.

1 · NotationSoil chips from the Munsell soil-chart pages; plant hues from the Plant Tissue book; calibration hues around the colour circle.
2 · Renotation dataMunsell's published measurements turn each notation into CIE xyY (Illuminant C).
3 · Print + screen valuesConverted to Lab (D50) for printing and measuring, and to sRGB for the artwork file.
4 · Gamut gateEach chip must print (CMYK round trip, ΔE2000 ≤ 3) and fit sRGB; if not, its chroma steps down on the same hue page.
5 · MeasureEach printed batch is measured with a spectrophotometer; measured values replace the design values in the app.

The scanned Munsell PDF's pixel colours were not used. It's a photograph of the chart, so its colours depend on the scanner. Only the notations and colour names were taken from it.

Cross-check: for the chips it covers, our sRGB values match Andrew Werth's Virtual Munsell Color Wheel within /255 per channel ( soil chips use odd chromas or half values that tool doesn't show). It is built from the same renotation data, so this checks our conversion, not the data itself (results/werth_crosscheck.txt).

Calibration row (A)

White and black anchors plus eight hues spread around the colour circle at the highest chroma that prints. Gives the colour-correction fit something to grip on in every direction.

Plant tissue (B–C)

Healthy leaf greens, chlorosis yellow-greens, senescence yellows/oranges, anthocyanin reds and purples, necrotic browns, white and tan roots. Every hue is a page of the Munsell Plant Tissue Color Book; the exact value/chroma are design targets to confirm with consultants.

Soil (D–E)

Twenty chips that exist on the soil-chart pages (10R to 2.5Y), each labelled with the name printed there. The low-chroma greys span the darkness of grey regoliths. No chip claims to match a specific simulant or Apollo sample.

Neutral ramp

Twelve greys from N9.5 to N1.5, including N5 (≈ 18 % grey), for white balance and the camera's tone curve.

Corner markers: which card is this?

The corners are standard ArUco markers from OpenCV's DICT_4X4_50 dictionary (the "4x4" option on chev.me/arucogen). The original card already used IDs ; v2 uses IDs . Any ArUco reader therefore knows which card is in the photo and which corner is which, even if the card is sideways or upside down.

IDs 0–3 were the best-separated block of four in the dictionary: the minimum bit differences from the v1 markers, between each other and from their own rotations are in the log below, so a misread is very unlikely. OpenCV's own detector reads both cards correctly (results/aruco_ids.txt):

Printing and measuring


    
  
  1. Print matte if possible: glare hides markers and chips (the app already has a fallback for photos where glare hides part of the card).
    2. 
  
  2. Measure at least 3 stickers from each batch, every chip, with a spectrophotometer (D50 / 2°). Record L*, a*, b* in
    the batch template.
    3. 
  
  3. Flag any chip whose sticker-to-sticker difference exceeds ΔE2000 2.
    4. 
  
  4. Feed the measured values to the generator (--measured); they replace the design values the app uses.

  5. Each print run gets its own batch number in the card's 8-cell code (3 bits version + 5 bits batch).
